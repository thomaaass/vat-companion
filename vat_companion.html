Five-year timeline

How the revenue is used from 2029–30 to 2033–34.

Sources: IFS (2025), Resolution Foundation (2025), HMRC ready reckoner; own growth assumptions.

How the 2029–30 figures are extended to other years
Starting point. The 2029-30 figures are those set out above: £54.7bn from the VAT rate change, £1.8bn from the threshold cut, and £24.5bn of stamp duty receipts. Every other year is derived from these. 1. VAT revenue grows 3.8% a year (own assumption). - Basis: HMRC's estimate of a one-point VAT change rises from £8.8bn (2026-27) to £9.2bn (2027-28) to £9.55bn (2028-29), and the IFS puts it at £9.9bn for 2029-30. The last two steps are 3.8% and 3.7%. Source: HMRC, "Direct effects of illustrative tax changes" (June 2025). https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/direct-effects-of-illustrative-tax-changes - Cross-check: the OBR's long-run assumptions of 1.75% real growth and 2.25% inflation imply about 4% nominal growth, so 3.8% is slightly cautious. - Applied to both the rate change and the threshold cut. - This assumes the split of consumer spending between standard-rated and zero-rated goods stays as it is in 2029-30. 2. Stamp duty receipts grow 6% a year (own assumption). - No published forecast was used beyond 2029-30. - Stamp duty grows faster than the economy because its thresholds are fixed in cash terms while house prices rise. The OBR's 2025 forecast had residential receipts rising from £11.5bn in 2025-26 to £19.7bn in 2030-31, about 11% a year; 6% assumes that pace slows once the housing market has recovered. - Sensitivity: the amount left over in 2033-34 is £34.7bn at 6%, £37.0bn at 4% and £32.3bn at 8%. 3. Years after 2031-32 are extrapolation. - The OBR's forecast ends in 2031-32. Figures for 2032-33 and 2033-34 simply continue the same growth rates and carry more uncertainty. 4. The one-off payment is fixed in cash. - £900 x 62.5 million people = £56.3bn, with the population held constant. - It is shown as two equal halves across 2029-30 and 2030-31 to show it being covered by the first two years of revenue. In practice it would be paid once. 5. Policy is held constant. - An 18% rate, a £30,000 threshold fully in place from the first year, and no stamp duty, in every year shown. 6. All figures are static. - No behavioural response is modelled in any year, and the one-off rise in the price level, with its effect on debt interest and benefit uprating, is not included.

Assumptions

Full price pass-through; households do not change what they buy. Spending is ONS 2024–25 patterns uplifted to 2029–30 prices with OBR CPI forecasts. Household composition is fixed. Stamp-duty savings and threshold effects are not allocated to deciles.
